# Launch on Duffel, then stack specialist suppliers

Duffel is the right Phase 1 choice. As of November 2026 it is the only travel supply API a startup with no booking history can sign up to on its own, publish prices against, and use to sell and ticket flights without its own IATA or ARC accreditation. It already offers flights, stays and, since April 2026, cars through one API. Amadeus closed its self-service portal on 17 July 2026 and Kiwi.com's Tequila has been invitation-only since 2024. Every other serious supplier (the GDSs, LCC aggregators, bedbanks, Expedia Rapid, Booking.com Demand) sits behind a sales contract, certification, a deposit or proof of volume. No single API reaches "all the data in the world". Duffel, the broadest startup-accessible source, does not cover activities, transfers or cruises. Its low-cost-carrier depth is unproven against specialists that claim around 400 LCCs. Vacation rentals at scale are locked inside Expedia (Vrbo, limited partners) and Booking.com (managed affiliates only). So maximum coverage comes from layering suppliers in stages, and each stage is unlocked by the booking history the previous one generates. The recommended stack runs from Duffel at launch to Viator, Mozio and affiliate gap-fillers in Phase 2, then a net-rate bedbank and an LCC aggregator, then Expedia Rapid and Booking.com once volume exists. ARC accreditation and a direct GDS come only at scale. Each step shifts more merchant-of-record, chargeback and seller-of-travel liability onto the business, so the legal setup has to grow with the supplier stack.

## No supplier covers the world, and their counts overlap rather than add

Every coverage figure in this market is a vendor's own marketing claim, and the claims conflict even within one company. Duffel's flight pages say "300+ airlines including 30+ Direct Connects" ([Duffel blog](https://duffel.com/blog/best-flight-booking-experience-british-airways-ndc)). Its April 2026 Cars launch post says **500+ airlines** and **about 1.6 million stay properties** ([Duffel blog](https://duffel.com/blog/introducing-duffel-cars)), while its homepage says "over 1 million properties" ([Duffel](https://duffel.com/)). Some of Duffel's GDS content comes through a Travelport deal ([Travolution](https://travolution.com/news/travel-sectors/air/duffel-strikes-travelport-deal-to-bring-more-choice-to-its-travel-merchants)), so its headline airline count leans on GDS-sourced fares rather than direct connections. LCC specialists claim more low-cost depth. Travelfusion's own pages variously say 370+, 400+ and 412+ LCC and scheduled airlines ([Travelfusion](https://corporate.travelfusion.com); [tfFlight API](https://corporate.travelfusion.com/products-services/tf-flight-api)). Mystifly claims **500+ airlines including 200+ LCCs** ([Mystifly](https://mystifly.com/?p=2071)). AltexSoft's survey of NDC aggregators warns that integrating any one of them "doesn't guarantee access to all carriers" ([AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/ndc-aggregators/)).

Stays have the same problem. The net-rate wholesalers (ETG/RateHawk at a claimed 2.5M–2.9M properties, TBO at 1M+, WebBeds at 500,000+) largely resell the same hotels, so their totals do not add up ([api-evangelist](https://github.com/api-evangelist/ratehawk); [ZentrumHub](https://www.zentrumhub.com/blog/ratehawk-hotel-api-guide/); [TBO](https://prep.tboholidays.com/hotels.aspx); [Channex](https://channex.io/integrations/webbeds)). The real gap is vacation rentals. **Vrbo on Expedia Rapid is "currently only available to a limited number of partners"** ([Expedia Developer Hub](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/vacation-rentals/vrbo-integration-guide)). Booking.com's Demand API, which carries its apartment and home inventory, requires a signed Managed Affiliate Partner contract ([Booking.com](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/getting-started/prerequisites)). Rentals United and NextPax are channel managers that connect property managers *to* OTAs ([Rentals United](https://info.rentalsunited.com/whitelabel-channel-manager-api); [NextPax](https://nextpax.com/resources/nextpax-supply-api-release-highlights)). Using them for demand would mean recruiting property managers one by one, which is a different business from plugging in an API. Duffel publishes no split of its stays inventory by property type. Its docs do mention properties with no front desk that need key-collection instructions ([Duffel Stays guide](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/getting-started-with-stays)), which points to some apartment supply, but how much is unknown.

## Duffel is the only self-serve path to selling flights without accreditation

The flight market has closed around Duffel. Amadeus confirmed in February 2026 that it was decommissioning the self-service side of Amadeus for Developers and paused registrations ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/amadeus-shut-down-self-service-apis-portal-developers)). The portal shut on **17 July 2026**, leaving only "request-based Enterprise APIs" with unclear terms ([Ignav](https://ignav.com/docs/amadeus-self-service-shutdown); [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/09/03/amadeus-brings-travel-tech-into-claude/)). Kiwi.com said in May 2024 that new Tequila partnerships would be "invitation only" ([Kiwi.com](https://media.kiwi.com/articles-and-interviews/better-for-business-kiwi-com-takes-a-new-approach-to-partnerships/)). Sabre binds API credentials to a Pseudo City Code and requires contract amendments for its main shopping API ([Sabre Dev Studio FAQ](https://developer.sabre.com/guides/travel-agency/faqs)). Travelport reportedly requires certification and a roughly $5,000 annual API fee ([supergood.ai](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/travelport)). Third parties put a direct GDS integration at **6–12 months** ([Tripgic](https://www.tripgic.com/playbook/best-travel-api/)). Mystifly onboards through commercial approval and vetting before it hands out a sandbox ([phptravels](https://phptravels.com/blog/mystifly-api-integration-guide)). Skyscanner is metasearch with redirect, so it is a place to list the OTA rather than a supply source ([Skyscanner Partner Support](https://skyscannerpartnersupport.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4687312848797-Which-services-can-I-access-and-what-are-the-rate-limits)).

Duffel's decisive feature is **Managed Content**. Sellers without IATA or ARC accreditation sell and ticket under Duffel's accreditations ([Duffel help](https://help.duffel.com/hc/en-gb/articles/360019644700-Do-I-need-to-be-an-ARC-IATA-travel-agent)), and Duffel "can ticket, service and settle Orders on your behalf" ([Duffel services agreement](https://duffel.com/services-agreement)). It is also the only flight supplier with a public price list: **$3.00 per order, 1% of order value for Managed Content, $2.00 per paid ancillary, a 1500:1 search-to-book allowance with $0.005 per excess search, 2% FX, and no upfront fees** ([Duffel pricing](https://duffel.com/pricing)). On a $400 ticket that works out to $3 + $4 = $7 in Duffel fees. Card processing through Duffel Payments, quoted at "an example would be 2.9%" ([Duffel docs](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/collecting-customer-card-payments)), adds about $11.60, for roughly $18.60 before markup. One fee needs confirming: an older help article puts the content fee at 0.5% ([Duffel help](https://help.duffel.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4409054162962)), against 1% on the live pricing page. The API covers seat maps, bags at and after booking, order changes, cancellations with refund quotes, and refunds to airline credits ([Duffel docs](https://docs.duffel.com); [Duffel changelog](https://changelog.duffel.com/announcements/refunds-to-airline-credits)). Availability varies by airline, so the UI needs a "contact support" fallback.

Duffel Stays and Duffel Cars complete the case for one Phase 1 contract. Stays runs a four-step search, rates, quote and book flow with a self-serve sandbox. Live Stays access has to be requested, and card payment needs prior approval ([Duffel Stays guide](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/getting-started-with-stays)). Duffel Cars launched in April 2026 with **40 providers, including Avis, Hertz, Sixt, Enterprise and Europcar, at 40,000+ locations in 200 countries** on the same API ([Duffel blog](https://duffel.com/blog/introducing-duffel-cars); [Business Travel News](https://www.businesstravelnews.com/Distribution/Duffel-Adds-Car-Rental-Booking-Capabilities)). Duffel says it is on track for $900m in transaction value in 2026, 85% of it from the US ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/duffel-hits-900m-run-rate-and-launches-cars-vertical/)), and Robinhood uses it for flights, hotels and cars ([Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/111185/robinhood-taps-duffel-to-power-travel-platform)). Those are useful signals that it can carry a US consumer brand.

Three weaknesses should be settled with Duffel's sales team before the Phase 1 spec is final. First, **Stays earnings are opaque**. The accommodation provider pays commission on completed stays and "Duffel passes a share to you", but no percentage is published and a booking only counts as complete once the guest checks out ([Duffel pricing](https://duffel.com/pricing)). Unit economics therefore cannot be modelled from public data, and revenue arrives weeks or months after the customer pays. Second, the LCCs that matter to US travellers (Spirit, Frontier, Allegiant, Southwest and others) need checking against Duffel's live airline list. No dated source confirmed them. Third, the exact merchant-of-record wording for Managed Content with Duffel Payments could not be retrieved. Duffel defines the merchant of record as "the company that collects payment from the customer" ([Duffel help](https://help.duffel.com/hc/en-gb/sections/4408837946898-Duffel-Payments)), and Duffel Payments "only works with Managed Content" ([Duffel docs](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/collecting-customer-card-payments)). On Stays, the business either pays from a pre-funded Duffel Balance or uses an approved card ([Duffel docs](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/collecting-and-making-payments)). If it collects through its own Stripe account and pays Duffel from the balance, it is the merchant of record for that stay.

## Wider hotels come from bedbanks; vacation rentals wait for Expedia and Booking.com

Once Phase 1 has run for a while, the best second stays source is a net-rate bedbank, because it adds both breadth and margin control. ETG/RateHawk's B2B API "uses net prices and you calculate your own markup", and it requires a signed agreement and possibly certification, with sandbox and production hosts ([ETG docs](https://docs.emergingtravel.com/docs/integration-guide/)). Hotelbeds issues free evaluation keys on registration ([HBX developer portal](https://developer.hotelbeds.com/)). Live access needs a commercial agreement, and startups should expect deposit or prepayment terms ([PHPTRAVELS](https://phptravels.com/blog/travel-api-suppliers)). Hotelbeds has one more advantage: the same developer account covers hotels, activities, transfers and cars, so one contract opens several Phase 2 verticals. WebBeds grants access only "following commercial agreement and technical approval" ([WebBeds](https://www.webbeds.com/buyers/support/)). Adding any second hotel source brings a hidden cost, because the same property arrives under different IDs and has to be mapped and deduplicated. Duffel's quote step does the same job as a bedbank's prebook, so a supplier-neutral booking flow in the Next.js server routes keeps that cost manageable.

Expedia Rapid and Booking.com Demand hold the deepest combined hotel and rental inventory, and both are partnerships judged on volume. Expedia describes Rapid as a revenue partnership in which applicants submit their business model and projected volume ([StayAPI](https://stayapi.com/blog/expedia-rapid-api)), and a third party puts certification at **3–6 months** ([OneClick IT Solution](https://www.oneclickitsolution.com/blog/top-hotel-booking-apis)). Even Expedia's inventory-opportunities feature asks new partners for **more than $50,000 net revenue in the last 3 months** ([Expedia Developer Hub](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/shopping/inventory-opportunities?locale=en_US)), which shows the scale Expedia expects. Vrbo on Rapid adds its own rules: partner virtual cards, a rental agreement shown at checkout, weekly content refreshes, and no packaging ([Vrbo integration guide](https://developers.expediagroup.com/rapid/lodging/vacation-rentals/vrbo-integration-guide)). Third parties say Booking.com's Demand API targets "businesses with real distribution" and that pre-launch startups "rarely clear the bar" ([StayAPI](https://stayapi.com/blog/booking-com-api)). Until then, affiliate links fill the rental and hostel gap with no payment risk. Booking.com pays about 4% of booking value via CJ/Awin and Agoda about 4–7%, though both figures come from aggregators rather than official terms ([AffiliateJob](https://affiliatejob.org/programs/booking); [Travelpayouts](https://www.travelpayouts.com/blog/best-hotel-affiliate-program/)). Trip.com lists 5.5% on hotels via Awin ([Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile/22405)).

## Activities, transfers and cars have startup-friendly routes; cruises do not

Activities offer the best low-risk middle ground of any vertical. Viator's affiliate Basic tier is free, with no traffic minimum, and pays **8% on bookings within 30 days** ([Viator Partner Resource Center](https://partnerresources.viator.com/)). Its **Full + Booking** tier lets customers check out on the partner's own site while "Viator is the merchant of record and handles customer service" ([Viator levels of access](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/levels-of-access/)). That gives the OTA a branded checkout without taking on refunds or chargebacks. It needs approval, certification and the /cart endpoints ([Viator technical guide](https://docs.viator.com/partner-api/technical/)). GetYourGuide (200,000+ experiences in 18,000 cities) offers a read-only API and a "Masterbill" booking tier in which the partner becomes merchant of record ([GetYourGuide help](https://partner.getyourguide.support/hc/en-us/articles/13981133907613-API-integration-and-requirements)). Klook is strongest in Asia-Pacific and pays 2–20% by category ([Involve Asia](https://involve.asia/blog/klook-affiliate-program/)). Tiqets, now owned by Expedia, sets a floor of "around 200 orders a month" for its Distributor API ([Tiqets](https://www.tiqets.com/partner-program/api-program); [Expedia IR](https://ir.expediagroup.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2025/Expedia-Group-Announces-Agreement-to-Acquire-Tiqets-to-Expand-Global-Activities-and-Experiences/default.aspx)). Expedia's own Rapid Activities API is in beta with a wider release planned for 2027 ([Expedia Group](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/landing-pages/make-every-trip-more-memorable-with-rapid-activities-api)). Holibob is the one-API alternative, aggregating 45+ supply partners through GraphQL, but its commercial terms are not public ([Holibob](https://holibob.tech)).

For transfers, **Mozio** is the strongest first choice. It covers 3,500+ airports and cities in 180+ countries through an API, white label or widget, lets agents set their own percentage or flat markup, and charges no sign-up fee ([Mozio](https://www.mozio.com/); [Mozio travel agents](https://www.mozio.com/travel-agents)). Welcome Pickups (350+ cities) is the premium meet-and-greet option, though it does not say who handles payment and customer service on API bookings ([Welcome Pickups](https://partner.welcomepickups.com/travel-api/)). Hoppa pays 5–6% through Awin as an affiliate fallback ([Awin](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/5511)). Booking.com's airport taxis are still "coming soon" ([Booking.com Demand](https://developers.booking.com/demand)).

Cars are now largely solved by Duffel Cars, which arrived after the original phase plan was drawn up. The broader car APIs are not yet open. Expedia's Rapid Car API is in beta, with full launch in "early 2027", and it makes **the partner the merchant of record** ([Expedia Rapid Car](https://partner.expediagroup.com/en-us/landing-pages/rapid-car-api-for-scalable-car-rental-bookings)). Booking.com's book-in-place cars flow needs "a valid business case", and its own docs disagree on whether it is production-ready or a pilot ([Booking.com Cars getting started](https://developers.booking.com/demand/docs/cars/getting-started)). Expedia agreed to buy CarTrawler in May 2026, so that B2B supply is moving inside Expedia ([Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2026/05/20/cartrawler-acquired-by-us-travel-group-expedia/)). Discover Cars' affiliate terms show a profit share of up to 70%, but the same document also lists a lower schedule, so check the rate on signup ([Discover Cars](https://www.discovercars.com/de/affiliate-conditions)).

Cruises are the one vertical with no startup-grade booking API. Specialist engines such as Traveltek (30+ bookable lines; the figure is from 2021) and Odysseus book through the agency's own cruise-line accounts ([Traveltek](https://www.traveltek.com/travel-api-provider/cruise-api)). The lines gate those accounts on accreditation: Norwegian requires an ARC, IATA or TRUE appointment letter or a CLIA certificate ([Norwegian Central](https://norwegiancentral.ncl.com/account)). The realistic early path is affiliate links: CruiseDirect pays 3% with no minimum sales, and Cruises.com pays 1.6% ([CruiseDirect](https://www.cruisedirect.com/faq/affiliates); [FlexOffers](https://www.flexoffers.com/affiliate-programs/cruises-com-affiliate-program)). Widgety's content API (72 lines) can power SEO-friendly cruise browse pages in the meantime ([Widgety](https://widgety.org/about)). Expedia Cruises is a franchise costing a $49,000 initial fee, not an API, so it does not suit a web-only OTA ([SharpSheets](https://sharpsheets.io/blog/expedia-cruises-franchise-fdd-profits-costs)).

## Each supplier choice decides who carries chargebacks, refunds and licensing

**Merchant of record** is the most consequential business decision in the stack. The merchant of record is whoever charges the card. Under the agency model the supplier charges the card and carries most chargeback risk. Under the merchant model the OTA earns margin but owns refunds, chargebacks and tax exposure ([AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/merchant-of-record-in-travel/); [OTA Playbook](https://otaplaybook.com/merchant-model-vs-agency-model-ota/)). Travel chargebacks often arrive months after the sale, because the dispute window runs from the travel date ([Payment Nerds](https://paymentnerds.com/blog/why-travel-chargebacks-can-show-up-months-after-the-sale/)). Card acquirers treat travel as high-risk and may impose reserves or rolling holdbacks ([PayKings](https://paykings.com/blog/travel-agency-merchant-accounts/)). On air, the US DOT rule requires the responsible airline or ticket agent to refund credit-card purchases within **7 business days**, and which party is responsible depends on who is merchant of record ([OTA Playbook](https://otaplaybook.com/merchant-model-vs-agency-model-ota/)). That is why the staged stack below starts with supplier-collect and affiliate models (Duffel Payments, Viator Full + Booking, affiliate links). It moves to self-collected net rates (bedbanks, Viator Merchant, GetYourGuide Masterbill, Expedia Rapid Car) only once customer-service operations and a payment processor relationship exist.

**Seller-of-travel registration** applies by where customers live, not where the company is based ([Host Agency Reviews](https://hostagencyreviews.com/blog/travel-agent-license-seller-of-travel-license)). A national US website selling to consumers therefore needs to register in California, Florida, Hawaii and Washington. Secondary 2026 guides give these costs: California about $100 per location per year plus $330 for the Travel Consumer Restitution Fund; Florida about $300 a year plus a **$25,000 bond**; Hawaii $215 in even years and $146 in odd years; Washington $222–$234, with sources disagreeing ([Atlas Coast Travel](https://www.atlascoasttravel.com/blog/seller-of-travel-laws-by-state); [Fora Travel](https://www.foratravel.com/join/resources/do-you-need-a-travel-agent-license); [Junglebee](https://junglebee.com/blog/seller-of-travel-laws-tour-operator-registration)). One guide says Maryland registration began rolling out in October 2026, which is unverified ([Fora Travel](https://www.foratravel.com/join/resources/do-you-need-a-travel-agent-license)). Confirm every figure with the state agencies and a travel lawyer before launch.

**ARC and IATA** are not needed in Phase 1. ARC is needed only to issue air tickets yourself. It requires a bond, letter of credit or deposit of at least **$20,000** (or $150,000 for a foreign-domiciled parent) and an application fee of about $2,000–$3,000 depending on the source ([ARC](https://www2.arccorp.com/support-training/airlines/information-on-agency-accreditation-for-airlines/); [ARC datasheet](https://www2.arccorp.com/globalassets/products--participation/travel-agencies/become-an-arc-agent/Accreditation-Datasheet_2020.pdf)), and processing takes about 90 days ([SuretyBonds.com](https://www.suretybonds.com/guide/federal/arc-accreditation)). US agencies cannot get IATA TIDS. Their non-ticketing ID is IATAN, at roughly $280 to apply and $120 a year ([AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/travel-agency-accreditation/); [Fora Travel](https://www.foratravel.com/join/resources/how-to-get-iata-number)). IATAN or CLIA becomes useful in a later phase because cruise lines and some supplier programmes require one of these credentials.

**PCI DSS v4.0.1** has been fully in force since 31 March 2025. Hosted payment fields or an iframe keep the business in the lightest questionnaire (SAQ A). A January 2025 revision added an eligibility condition: the merchant must confirm the payment page is not susceptible to script attacks, either by applying script inventory and tamper detection or by getting written confirmation from its PCI-compliant provider ([Hyperproof](https://hyperproof.io/resource/pci-dss-4-0-update-new-saq-a-eligibility-criteria/)). Experts disagree on whether that protection covers only the payment page or the whole site ([TrustedSec](https://trustedsec.com/blog/the-hidden-trap-in-the-pci-dss-saq-a-changes); [Akamai](https://www.akamai.com/blog/security/pci-dss-v4-0-1-changes-qualify-saq-a)). On a Next.js site with marketing scripts, the safe reading is to keep checkout pages free of third-party scripts. If the site later sells to UK or EU customers, bundling a flight with a hotel can make the business a package organiser, with ATOL or insolvency-protection duties ([Travelgenix](https://www.travelgenix.io/guides/atol-explained)).

## A staged stack trades control for coverage as volume unlocks access

The sequence below follows the access gates documented above. Each phase uses suppliers the business can realistically win with the booking history it has at that point.

| Phase | Trigger | Add | Model and liability |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Launch | Now | Duffel Flights (Managed Content), Duffel Stays, and Duffel Cars after confirming access | Duffel tickets under its accreditation. Confirm the merchant of record in the contract. Register in CA, FL, HI and WA; use hosted card fields |
| 2. Fill the trip | First months of bookings | Viator Basic, then Full + Booking (activities); Mozio (transfers); affiliate links for cruises (CruiseDirect), rentals and hostels (Booking.com, Agoda), and extra activities (GetYourGuide, Klook) | Viator and the affiliates stay merchant of record. Mozio adds your own markup |
| 3. Widen and add margin | Steady volume, company able to fund deposits | ETG/RateHawk or Hotelbeds (hotels, plus Hotelbeds activities and transfers); Travelfusion or Mystifly for LCCs; Widgety cruise content | You become merchant of record on net rates. Needs hotel mapping and dedupe, fraud tools and a support desk |
| 4. Unlock the gated tail | Proof of volume (Expedia's own language points to tens of thousands of dollars a quarter) | Expedia Rapid (with Vrbo, if admitted), Booking.com Demand, Expedia Rapid Car and Activities once generally available, the Tiqets Distributor API (~200 orders a month) | Contract-specific. Price-display and launch rules apply |
| 5. Scale | Air margin justifies the bond | ARC plus IATAN or CLIA; a direct GDS (Sabre or Travelport); cruise engines (Traveltek or Odysseus) on your own line accounts | Full ticketing liability, a $20k+ bond and a 6–12 month GDS build |

This path reaches near-maximum coverage, but never total coverage. Each added supplier brings fewer new products than the last, plus more mapping, reconciliation and support work. That makes the order of the phases as important as the list.

## Conclusion

The question is not which API has the most data. It is which API a business with no booking history is allowed to use. Supplier access in 2026 has narrowed: Amadeus and Kiwi have closed their open doors, and Expedia, Booking.com and CarTrawler are consolidating B2B supply behind volume-judged partnerships. Duffel's self-serve model is therefore both the best Phase 1 option and close to the only one. Its real value is that it lets the business build the booking record every other supplier asks for. The Phase 1 spec should go ahead on Duffel, with three items confirmed in writing before build: the Stays profit-share rate, the merchant-of-record and refund responsibilities under Duffel Payments, and coverage of the key US low-cost carriers.

The architecture matters as much as the contracts. Building a supplier-neutral search, quote and book layer in the Next.js server from day one costs little now. It turns every later supplier into an adapter rather than a rebuild, and the dedupe and mapping work that multi-sourcing requires has a place to live. On the business side, the biggest risk is liability rather than inventory: every move from affiliate or supplier-collect models to self-collected net rates adds chargebacks, refund deadlines and bonding. Margin should be bought one vertical at a time, and only once customer-service capacity exists.
