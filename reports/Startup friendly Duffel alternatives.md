# Keep Duffel flights, add LiteAPI for stays

Duffel is still the best starting point for flights, and in practice the only self-serve one. A brand-new global OTA with no IATA or ARC accreditation and no booking history has no comparable alternative for air in November 2026. Amadeus shut its self-service portal on 17 July 2026, Kiwi.com's Tequila is partnership-only, and every remaining flight source that would let the site take bookings itself is a consolidator: Mystifly, PKFARE, TBO, AERTiCKET or Travelfusion. Each needs KYC or a signed contract, quotes its prices privately, and makes the startup fund a wallet or deposit and charge the traveller's card itself. Stays is different. **LiteAPI (now Nuitée Connect) is easier to get into than Duffel Stays.** You get a sandbox key at signup and production as soon as you add a card. You set your own margin. Payouts are weekly. Nuitée offers to act as merchant of record through its payment SDK. Duffel Stays needs a contact-form request, card payments need separate approval, and its profit share is unpublished. The recommendation is to launch on **Duffel for flights and LiteAPI as the primary stays source**. Request Duffel Stays access at the same time as a second source, put both behind a supplier-neutral adapter, and pick the long-term primary after a short paid pilot. Leave consolidators for a later phase, once the business has cash for deposits and its own payments and chargeback operation. The weakest link in this plan is evidence, not access. No independent user reviews of LiteAPI were found, and the merchant-of-record and chargeback terms for both Duffel and Nuitée still have to be confirmed in writing.

## Amadeus's exit left Duffel as the only self-serve flight API

The open doors in air distribution have closed one by one. Amadeus confirmed in February 2026 that it would decommission its self-service APIs, and keys stopped working on **17 July 2026**. Only request-based Enterprise access remains, with no stated terms ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/amadeus-shut-down-self-service-apis-portal-developers); [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/09/03/amadeus-brings-travel-tech-into-claude/)). Kiwi.com's Tequila stopped self-service registration in 2024 ([Ignav](https://ignav.com/docs/kiwi-tequila-alternative)). Duffel's **Managed Content** "allows those without an IATA or ARC accreditation to start selling flights fast by using Duffel's established ticketing authority" ([Duffel Help](https://help.duffel.com/hc/en-gb/articles/9066583986706-What-is-Managed-Content)). It is still the only flight product found that combines open signup, published prices and ticketing under someone else's accreditation. No new self-serve "Duffel clone" turned up in this research.

The alternatives fall into four groups, and only the first lets the startup take a paid booking on its own site. **Consolidators** ticket under their own BSP or ARC accreditation for non-IATA agents, for a fee ([AltexSoft](https://altexsoft.com/blog/booking-ticketing-non-iata-travel-agencies)).

Travelfusion's registration is a sales questionnaire. Its API use is "subject to fees as set out in our license agreement", which must be signed first. Customers using its TFPay deposit option put down **EUR/GBP 5,000** up front ([Travelfusion](https://corporate.travelfusion.com/resources/register-customer-supplier); [Travelfusion docs](https://xmldocs.travelfusion.com/travelfusion-direct-connect-xml-api/getting-started-guide); [TFPay deposit](https://xmldocs.travelfusion.com/travelfusion-direct-connect-xml-api/guidelines/tfpay-payment-travel-innovation-settlement/tfpay-deposit)).

Mystifly reportedly onboards through KYC, then commercial approval, then test and production credentials. Only integrator sites describe this ([phptravels](https://phptravels.com/blog/mystifly-api-integration-guide)).

PKFARE has a buyer sign-up, but an integrator says "PKFARE decides who gets access" ([PKFARE](https://www.pkfare.com/); [phptravels](https://phptravels.com/pkfare-api-integration)). TBO's application asks whether you are IATA or non-IATA. Its own docs describe test-case certification taking about **4–5 working days** before live access ([TBO Air certification](https://dealint.tboair.com/APIDocument/Certification.aspx)). AERTiCKET markets its Cockpit API to "all travel agencies, whether IATA or non-IATA" but publishes no docs ([TourMaG](https://www.tourmag.com/AERTICKET-simplifie-l-activite-des-professionnels-du-tourisme_a121599.html)).

The other three groups look useful but fail the brief. AirGateway is the only alternative with a public price list, but its signup form asks for an IATA number ([AirGateway pricing](https://airgateway.com/pricing)). Hahnair's **Distriply One** is built for "independent and non-accredited travel sellers" with "no upfront investment". It is an agent-facing booking tool, though, and no API is mentioned ([Hahnair](https://www.hahnair.com/en/press-release/8418/2025-10-01/hahnairs-new-ndc-booking-platform-distriply-one-is-live-and-ready-for-travel-consultants-in-the-us)). Travelpayouts White Label and Trip.com affiliate links are redirects. On Travelpayouts the site "does not issue airline tickets", and the commission is 1–7% ([Travelpayouts](https://special.travelpayouts.com/wltravelagency)). Hopper's HTS signs only large brands such as Capital One and WestJet ([HTS newsroom](https://hts.hopper.com/news)). Mondee filed for Chapter 11 in January 2025, sold its assets to its lenders, and was renamed Tabhi, so it is a risk to avoid until proven otherwise ([Skift](https://skift.com/2025/01/15/booking-platform-mondee-files-for-bankruptcy); [PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/mondee-acquired-chapter-11-restructuring-tabhi)).

The consolidators are complements, not substitutes. Their coverage leans in different directions: PKFARE and TBO towards Asia and India, AERTiCKET towards European BSP markets, Travelfusion towards European low-cost carriers. That makes them useful later for filling specific gaps in Duffel's results. Duffel's own LCC depth is the one place a consolidator could win at launch. No dated source confirmed that Duffel sells Spirit, Frontier, Allegiant, Southwest, Ryanair or Wizz, so check its live airline list against the target markets before ruling a consolidator in or out.

## Consolidators move payments and chargebacks onto the startup

The deciding difference between Duffel and the consolidators is not inventory. It is who touches the traveller's money. With a consolidator, the startup pays the consolidator from a prepaid wallet, deposit or credit line, then charges the traveller through its own payment gateway. PKFARE bookings run on "markup and wallet balance" ([phptravels](https://phptravels.com/pkfare-api-integration)). TBO's API includes a wallet-balance check ([Scribd, TBO API](https://www.scribd.com/document/712249084/AuthenticationAPI-SearchAPI-WalletBalanceCheckAPI)). Mystifly leaves payments, markups and invoices to the seller's own booking engine ([phptravels](https://phptravels.com/blog/mystifly-api-integration-guide)). That makes the startup the merchant of record to the customer. Travelport's guidance says the consolidator holds "ultimate ticketing authority and financial responsibility" toward the airline ([AltexSoft](https://www.altexsoft.com/blog/travelport-api-integration/)). That responsibility does not extend to consumer card disputes. Refunds also run slowly: the consolidator refunds the agent only after the airline refunds the consolidator ([AltexSoft](https://altexsoft.com/blog/booking-ticketing-non-iata-travel-agencies)). Meanwhile, in the US the responsible ticket agent must refund card purchases within **7 business days** ([OTA Playbook](https://otaplaybook.com/merchant-model-vs-agency-model-ota/)). A startup on a consolidator can therefore owe a traveller a refund before it has received the money back itself.

Duffel removes most of that exposure at launch. Its services agreement says Duffel "can ticket, service and settle Orders on your behalf" ([Duffel services agreement](https://duffel.com/services-agreement)). **Duffel Payments** collects the traveller's card, "an example would be 2.9%", and it "only works with Managed Content" ([Duffel docs](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/collecting-customer-card-payments)). Duffel defines the merchant of record as "the company that collects payment from the customer" ([Duffel help](https://help.duffel.com/hc/en-gb/sections/4408837946898-Duffel-Payments)). On that definition Duffel takes the processing role for the fare. The contract wording on who carries chargebacks for the startup's markup, though, was not retrieved. On a $400 ticket, Duffel's published fees come to $3 plus $4 (1%), or $7, plus about $11.60 in card processing. That is roughly **$18.60 before the startup's markup**. One discrepancy needs settling: an older help article puts the content fee at 0.5% rather than 1% ([Duffel pricing](https://duffel.com/pricing); [Duffel help](https://help.duffel.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4409054162962)). The consolidator equivalent cannot be costed at all, because no rate card, wallet minimum or merchant-of-record clause was published for Mystifly, PKFARE, TBO or AERTiCKET.

The case for a consolidator at launch is the strongest one against Duffel, and it deserves a fair hearing. Net B2B fares with a markup can beat Duffel's price on some routes, especially in Asia and on LCCs, and they could earn more per ticket. That only pays off once the business has working capital for a wallet, a card processor willing to accept airline-ticket risk, and a support desk for voids and refunds. A pre-revenue startup has none of these.

## LiteAPI is the one stays API as open as Duffel

Stays has a real alternative, unlike flights. LiteAPI's signup is "create a free account at connect.nuitee.com, copy your sandbox API key", then "add a valid credit card under Payment Methods to unlock your production key". No deposit, minimum or approval step is mentioned ([Nuitée FAQ](https://docs.nuitee.com/docs/faq)). The product pitches "a self-serve API and sandbox to move fast from day one" to startups ([Nuitée Connect](https://nuitee.com/connect)). The old liteapi.travel domain now redirects to Nuitée, so the two names refer to the same product ([liteapi.travel](https://www.liteapi.travel/pricing)). Duffel Stays, by contrast, gives a self-serve sandbox. Live access, though, means asking: "Request access to Duffel Stays" through a contact form, plus separate approval before taking card payments ([Duffel Stays guide](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/getting-started-with-stays); [Duffel docs](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/collecting-customer-card-payments)).

Every other stays option is either gated or not a booking API. ZentrumHub claims "no minimum sales requirements". It is connectivity software, not supply, though. Plans start around **$650 a month**, and its own FAQ estimates **$5,000–$10,000 in deposits per supplier** you contract ([ZentrumHub FAQ](https://www.zentrumhub.com/faq)). Travelgate says "Connect for free" to startups. Buyers still need credentials from each seller and must build and certify, under a success-fee model with no published rates ([Travelgate](https://www.travelgate.com/solutions/startup)). Stuba releases its test API at registration but its live URL only after certification ([Stuba](https://developer.stuba.com/hapi/getting-started)). RateHawk and Hotelbeds publish no deposit or credit terms. Integrators describe a contract-first process in which new entrants face "higher scrutiny" ([phptravels RateHawk](https://phptravels.com/integrations/ratehawk-api-integration); [phptravels Hotelbeds](https://phptravels.com/hotelbeds-api-xml-integration)). For hostels and villas, the open routes are affiliate ones. Hostelworld is free to join on a cost-per-acquisition basis ([Hostelworld partners](https://partners.hostelworld.com/faqs)), and Holidu has an Affiliate API for vacation-rental search ([Holidu developer](https://developer.holidu.com/reference/intro-1)). Several once-common routes are gone. Hotellook's API stopped working after Travelpayouts closed it in October 2025 ([Travelpayouts](https://support.travelpayouts.com/hc/en-us/articles/29534131568530-FAQ-on-the-closure-of-Hotellook)). Airbnb reportedly is "not accepting new access requests" for its API ([bnbcalc](https://www.bnbcalc.com/help/articles/does-airbnb-have-an-api)). An API health checker lists Impala as dead ([API Bouncer](https://www.apibouncer.com/apis/1038/impala-hotel-bookings)).

## LiteAPI wins on economics and payments, Duffel on track record

LiteAPI's commercial model is the most transparent found for stays. The seller sets a percentage `margin` per request, with "no hidden platform fees on that commission". A margin of zero returns net rates ([Nuitée revenue doc](https://docs.nuitee.com/docs/revenue-management-and-commission)). Core booking and management endpoints are free. Some premium endpoints are billed separately, at prices not captured here ([Nuitée FAQ](https://docs.nuitee.com/docs/faq)). The payment model matters as much as the price. **When the startup uses Nuitée's payment SDK or white label, "Nuitee as merchant of record" processes the guest's payment**, and the startup's margin is built into the price. Alternatively, the startup can act as merchant itself on net rates ([Nuitée revenue doc](https://docs.nuitee.com/docs/revenue-management-and-commission)). Payouts go out weekly, but only for confirmed bookings, and a booking counts as confirmed "when a guest completes their stay and checks out". Like Duffel's, LiteAPI's revenue therefore lags the sale. As a rough worked example, a 10–15% margin on a $300 stay earns $30–45, against about $12–18 from a 4–6% affiliate commission. That assumes the marked-up price still converts against Booking.com, which was not tested.

Duffel Stays cannot yet be modelled the same way. "The accommodation provider pays the commission on completed bookings. Duffel passes a share to you", and no percentage is published ([Duffel pricing](https://duffel.com/pricing)). The startup pays Duffel from a pre-funded Duffel Balance or an approved card. If it collects from guests through its own Stripe account, it is the merchant of record for that stay ([Duffel payments guide](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/collecting-and-making-payments)). So with LiteAPI the startup can avoid running a hotel payments stack at launch, while with Duffel Stays it most likely cannot. That gap is the strongest single reason to choose LiteAPI. It depends on one documentation page, and the chargeback terms behind it were not found.

Duffel's advantages are real, and they are the reason not to drop it from stays entirely. One contract, one dashboard and one support line cover flights and stays. Duffel is on track for **$900m in transaction value in 2026** and powers Robinhood's travel product ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/duffel-hits-900m-run-rate-and-launches-cars-vertical/); [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/pressarticle/111185/robinhood-taps-duffel-to-power-travel-platform)). A third-party API report card rates Duffel "Grade A" ([Supergood](https://supergood.ai/api-report-card/duffel)). LiteAPI has no equivalent public signal. A search for independent reviews returned only vendor material and API directories ([apis.io](https://apis.io/apis/nuitee/nuitee-hotel-data-api/)). Its default rate limit is **5 requests per second**, and support runs through Intercom and Discord ([Nuitée FAQ](https://docs.nuitee.com/docs/faq)). Both are fine for a pilot but need confirming before marketing spend drives traffic. LiteAPI also sets a floor price, the suggested selling price (SSP), below which rates may not be shown publicly. Lower prices are allowed only behind a login, in an app or inside a package ([Nuitée revenue doc](https://docs.nuitee.com/docs/revenue-management-and-commission)). That limits discounting, but it also gives a flight-plus-hotel bundle a legitimate way to price below public rates. Nuitée also advertises flights from "600+ airlines" ([Nuitée Connect](https://nuitee.com/connect)). Its flights sandbox does not mirror production, though ([Nuitée FAQ](https://docs.nuitee.com/docs/faq)), and nothing found shows it ticketing for non-accredited sellers. It is no reason to move flights off Duffel.

The integration cost of pairing them is modest. Both use the same search, rates, quote or prebook, then book sequence ([Duffel Stays guide](https://duffel.com/docs/guides/getting-started-with-stays); [Nuitée FAQ](https://docs.nuitee.com/docs/faq)). One adapter interface in the Next.js server routes can serve either supplier. The real extra work comes when both stays sources run at once: matching the same hotel across two ID systems and removing duplicates. One more cost arrives later. If the site sells flight-and-hotel bundles to UK or EU customers, it can become a package organiser with insolvency-protection duties ([Travelgenix](https://www.travelgenix.io/guides/atol-explained)). That applies whichever stays supplier sits underneath.

## The launch stack, and what each provider must confirm in writing

The recommended launch stack has three parts. Duffel Managed Content with Duffel Payments handles flights. LiteAPI, through Nuitée's payment SDK, is the primary stays source. Duffel Stays access is requested in parallel. Run a two-to-four-week paid pilot that books the same properties through both stays suppliers and compares rates, confirmation reliability and support response. Make Duffel Stays the primary only if its profit share and payment terms beat Nuitée's in writing. Holidu's affiliate API or Hostelworld links can fill villas and hostels without payment risk. Consolidators come in Phase 2, picked for specific gaps the pilot exposes: PKFARE or TBO for Asia, Travelfusion or AERTiCKET for European LCCs, Mystifly for breadth. Each brings a wallet or deposit and moves card risk onto the startup.

Live coverage of the LCCs that matter in each target market. Who emails e-tickets and handles traveller servicing. |
| Duffel (stays) | How long Stays access approval takes. The profit-share percentage and payout timing. Card-payment approval criteria. Whether the 2% FX fee applies. Upstream suppliers and the share of apartments and villas. |

## Conclusion

Narrowing access has changed which question matters. A year ago the choice was which API had the most content. In late 2026 it is which supplier will let an unproven company take money from a traveller without first building a payments, refunds and chargeback operation. On that test, Duffel wins flights by default and LiteAPI edges Duffel on stays, because Nuitée offers to be merchant of record and publishes its margin rules. The consolidators that look cheaper on fares cost more in practice, because their deposits, card risk and slow refunds land on the startup.

The pairing is a hedge against platform risk as much as an economic choice. Amadeus showed in July 2026 that a self-serve channel can disappear within months. Running two stays suppliers behind one adapter from day one means neither Duffel nor Nuitée can cut off the site's hotel supply. The pilot bookings also give the business the history that the gated bedbanks, Expedia and Booking.com ask for before they will sign.
