# travel-booking: reference cards

Scout, 2026-11-10. Nine finalists, all from screening passes. I visited each one myself at 1440x900 and 390x844 in desktop Chromium, with the scrollbar hidden. 390 in desktop Chromium is not a phone: hover rules still run, and sites that detect the device may serve their desktop layout (Ferryhopper did). Fonts were read from computed styles and from the font files each page loaded. Checkout and account pages were not visited on any site, because they need a search form or a sign-in. Screens are in references/screens/.

Channel mix: 6 niche (adjacent businesses), 3 cross-niche, 0 from galleries.

## 1. Hipcamp (https://www.hipcamp.com/en-GB)
- Found via: niche search, adjacent business (camping and glamping stays), search 20
- Role: primary candidate, ranked 1
- What it is: a marketplace for booking campsites, cabins and glamping, with search, results, listing pages, checkout and a Trips area
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: the hero is a tabbed pill search (Destination / Roadtrip tabs, then Where, Dates, Campers and a round search button). The tabs map straight onto Flights / Hotels. Results are a list with filter chips beside a sticky price-pin map, and every card ends in "from £90 / night" and a Reserve button. An app-style left rail (Explore, Saves, Trips, Inbox) becomes a floating bottom tab bar at 390, a ready model for the account and bookings area.
  - Media: white ground with photo cards. Stock photos and Duffel hotel photos would sit in its cards without changing the system. Flight results would use the list half without the map.
  - Mood: calm, light, quietly confident, with one warm accent (orange search button, olive Reserve).
- What to take: the tabbed pill search, the list-plus-map results split with chip filters, the result-card anatomy (photo carousel, title, rating, place, amenity icons, price line, one action), the linked hover (the card tints warm and its map pin turns dark), and the app shell: left rail on desktop, floating bottom tab bar and bottom-sheet menu on mobile.
- How the client slots in: placeholder wordmark at the top of the rail; placeholder palette replaces the orange and olive; stock photos on homepage cards; Duffel photos in hotel results; airline logos on flight rows; Trips becomes My bookings.
- Risks: the listing detail page and checkout were not visited. The map needs a map provider and only suits hotels. The homepage below the search is a long grid of region photos that needs real destination content. A "Support" pill floats bottom right (no chat in Phase 1).
- Fonts: Calibre (Klim Type Foundry): commercial. Geist (Vercel): free, SIL OFL.
- Anti-slop: two tells, neither structural. The display headline ends in a full stop ("Camp your way."). One section is a row of three illustrated blobs with text. Breadcrumbs on the results page are allowed there (deep page). Do not carry the tells over.
- Estimated design quality and originality: 7 / 5 (two tells cap originality at 5)

## 2. Kasa (https://kasa.com)
- Found via: niche search, adjacent business (hotel operator), search 17
- Role: primary candidate, ranked 2. It has the strongest hotel detail page in the set.
- What it is: a US operator of 75+ boutique hotels and apartment hotels, booked direct
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: a four-field search bar in the hero (Destinations, Dates, Rooms & guests, Lowest rate, then a navy Search button), pinned as a compact bar at the top of inner pages. The property page runs: photo grid, description and amenities, "Apartments (13)" room cards with their own filters and "Check availability", highlights, a policy accordion (check-in, access, parking, accessibility), reviews, map, similar properties, FAQ. That is the brief's hotel detail page almost line for line.
  - Media: a looping hero video and interior photography. Stock video or a still would work, but this is the heaviest media need of the three primaries.
  - Mood: calm and trustworthy: navy (#061332) on a warm off-white ground (#F7F6F2).
- What to take: the property-page order, the room-card anatomy (carousel arrows appear on hover), the pinned compact search on inner pages, the policy accordion, the review cards with a source badge, the navy footer.
- How the client slots in: Duffel Stays photos fill the photo grid and room cards; Duffel's cancellation policy and rate rules fill the accordion; the placeholder palette replaces navy.
- Risks: the results list was not visited (homepage links go to cities and properties; the search needs a form). Nothing to borrow for flights. The video hero is darker than the brief's mood.
- Fonts: Tiempos Headline (Klim): commercial. TT Norms Pro (TypeType): commercial. Roboto loads only inside the Google Map: free.
- Anti-slop: a small eyebrow above every homepage section ("Our featured properties", "The Kasa experience", "Trusted by guests", "Groups & corporate stays", "Featured cities", "Our locations"): the AI eyebrow tell. The property FAQ sets its heading on the left and the questions on the right. "Seamless" appears in copy (copy tell).
- Estimated design quality and originality: 7 / 5
- Screens: kasa-first.png (screener); kasa-1440-hero.png, kasa-1440-full.png, kasa-1440-detail-full.png, kasa-1440-room-default.png, kasa-1440-room-hover.png, kasa-390-hero.png, kasa-390-menu.png

## 3. European Sleeper (https://www.europeansleeper.eu)
- Found via: niche search, adjacent business (rail), search 5 (Q42 case study)
- Role: primary candidate, ranked 3; also a donor for the route map and the trust footer
- What it is: a Dutch-Belgian night-train start-up that sells its own tickets
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: a transport search bar (Departure station, Arrival station, Outward, Return, Travel group, Search tickets) docked on the hero's rounded lower edge, a row of reasons under it, destination cards priced "From €49,99", a stylised route map, priced product cards, testimonials, a mission band, newsletter, footer. Booking runs in a separate ticket shop that was not visited.
  - Media: photo-led (life on the train). Stock travel photos could fill it; the route map shows place without photos.
  - Mood: warm and confident, but saturated plum, lilac and orange, so not light. The palette would be replaced anyway.
- What to take: bands that rise over each other with large rounded corners (close to Varun's layout move 7), the search bar docked on the hero edge, the route map as a model for a flight route arc or itinerary graphic, price-led destination cards with a slow photo zoom on hover, and a footer that names the legal entity, address, registration number and payment methods (trust for a new name).
- How the client slots in: airports replace stations; the placeholder palette replaces plum and orange; the route map becomes a custom illustration of the client's routes.
- Risks: checkout not verified. At 390 a newsletter popup covers the search card on load. Ambit's licence not confirmed.
- Fonts: Ambit (headings): unknown licence, not an open font I know of. GT Ultra (Grilli Type): commercial.
- Anti-slop: clean in what I saw. No eyebrows, no full-stop headlines, no em dashes on the homepage. The popup covering content at 390 is a craft issue, not a tell.
- Estimated design quality and originality: 7 / 7
- Screens: european-sleeper-first.png (screener); european-sleeper-1440-full.png, european-sleeper-1440-card-default.png, european-sleeper-1440-card-hover.png, european-sleeper-390-hero.png, european-sleeper-390-menu.png

## 4. Ferryhopper (https://www.ferryhopper.com/en/)
- Found via: niche search, adjacent business (ferries), search 21
- Role: section donor for the flight search card and an illustrated hero
- What it is: a ferry booking platform covering 360 operators in 63 countries
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: the closest flight-search model in the set. One way / Return / Multi-destination tabs, From and To with a swap button, stacked Depart and Return fields, passengers, pets and vehicles as an icon row, one full-width Search button. Results were not visited.
  - Media: a custom flat illustration (sea, islands, a ferry's red bow) runs under the search card: a model for illustration instead of stock.
  - Mood: light, white and mint, trustworthy.
- What to take: the search card's field anatomy and trip-type tabs, and an illustrated scene running under the search.
- How the client slots in: airports replace ports; passengers and cabin class replace pets and vehicles; our own illustration of the client's world, never the Ferryhopper scene.
- Risks: the lower homepage is template-like. At 390 in desktop Chromium the page served a 992px desktop layout and overflowed, so the real mobile layout is unverified (it probably depends on device detection). The search includes a Booking.com accommodation toggle (a competitor). A chat launcher and a feedback tab float on the page. No hover pair was captured for this site.
- Fonts: Averta (Kostas Bartsokas): commercial. Roboto: free.
- Anti-slop: a row of three illustrated icon cards ("Trusted by millions", "Travel at the best price", "Putting travelers first"), an operator logo carousel, a chat bubble.
- Estimated design quality and originality: 6 / 5
- Screens: ferryhopper-first.png, ferryhopper-route.png (screener); ferryhopper-1440-hero.png, ferryhopper-1440-section2.png, ferryhopper-390-hero.png

## 5. Mercury (https://mercury.com)
- Found via: cross-niche (neobanks and fintech, brief analogy 2)
- Role: section donor for the account dashboard (My bookings) and for price display
- What it is: US business banking for start-ups
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: a hero with a working email input and one button. On scroll the landscape pushes in to a laptop showing the product dashboard: a balance with superscript cents, an accounts list with right-aligned amounts, and small summary cards.
  - Media: one landscape scene; the product UI is the real media.
  - Mood: calm, premium, confident. Later sections go dark.
- What to take: the dashboard composition for My bookings (next trip card, bookings list, totals), price figures with superscript cents and right alignment, the plain disclosure pill at the foot of the hero as a model for a trust line (who the company is, who takes the payment), the quiet button hover (darkens over 0.3s).
- How the client slots in: fares and booking totals use the cents style; the client's legal entity and Stripe go in the disclosure line once known (placeholders until then).
- Risks: dark-heavy page, big brand, licensed fonts.
- Fonts: Arcadia Display and Arcadia Text (variable): unknown licence, appears custom or commercial. Tiempos Headline (Klim): commercial.
- Anti-slop: an em dash in the top announcement banner (copy tell). No design tells in the hero or the first product screen.
- Estimated design quality and originality: 8 / 7

## 6. Monocle City Guides (https://monocle.com/travel-guides/)
- Found via: cross-niche (editorial travel guides, brief analogy 5)
- Role: section donor for popular destinations as illustrated city cards, and for editorial type
- What it is: a global affairs and culture magazine; its City Guides section
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: a grid of cards, each with a series label in serif caps, a hairline rule, the city name in bold sans, and a commissioned illustration of the city. One card per row at 390.
  - Media: illustrations replace destination photos. That is the brief's own answer to missing media: custom illustration.
  - Mood: confident and editorial on this page; the homepage is busy and carries ads.
- What to take: the illustrated destination card system for the homepage's popular routes, the label, rule and name hierarchy, the underline-on-hover, and the large serif text menu at 390.
- How the client slots in: we draw or commission our own city illustrations in the client's palette, for routes the client chooses. The card label carries a fact (for example a "from" price), never a restated heading.
- Risks: the illustrations are David Doran's: never copy or trace them. Illustrating many cities is costly on a $3,000 budget, so keep it to four to six. Big media brand.
- Fonts: Plantin (Monotype): commercial. Helvetica Neue (Monotype): commercial. Open Sans also loads: free.
- Anti-slop: none in the City Guides grid. The homepage's pipe-separated section nav is close to the dot-separated keyword tell; do not carry it.
- Estimated design quality and originality: 7 / 8
- Screens: monocle-first.png (screener); monocle-1440-hero.png, monocle-1440-guides.png, monocle-1440-guide-hover.png, monocle-390-guides.png, monocle-390-menu.png

## 7. Sidetracked (https://www.sidetracked.com)
- Found via: cross-niche (editorial travel magazines, brief analogy 5), search 18
- Role: section donor for plain text pages (About, Terms, Privacy) and any article-style content
- What it is: an adventure travel magazine, online since 2011
- Fit with the brief:
  - Structure: article pages run: full-bleed photo header with title and subtitle, a centered byline block, a short rule, a bold serif standfirst, a dotted rule, then serif body in an 864px column at 19px with 30.4px leading.
  - Media: photo-led everywhere; for us only the text system matters.
  - Mood: quiet and literate on article pages; dark and moody on the homepage.
- What to take: the text-page system: column width, standfirst, byline block, rules, body size and leading. Optionally, the homepage's three story tabs with a progress bar as an idea for rotating popular routes.
- How the client slots in: About and the legal pages use this column with the client's type, under a plain band instead of a photo.
- Risks: a dark strip about 15px wide shows at the right edge on desktop (an inner scrollbar gutter); the wordmark runs edge to edge at 390; Adobe Fonts subscription.
- Fonts: Superior Title, Freight Text Pro, Proxima Nova (all via Adobe Fonts): commercial.
- Anti-slop: clean. Article subtitles are set wholly in italic (the whole line, not an accent word), which is fine but must not turn into italic accent words in headlines.
- Estimated design quality and originality: 8 / 7

## 8. Faye (https://www.withfaye.com)
- Found via: niche search, adjacent business (travel insurance), search 11
- Role: section donor for fare and room inclusions (checklist cards)
- What it is: a US travel insurance start-up with an app

  - Mood: friendly, warm and trustworthy: cream (#FEFEFA) and plum.

- Anti-slop: small eyebrows above most sections ("Features", "What's your trip type?", "Good words travel far"); a giant solid "BON VOYAGE" wordmark band before the footer, close to the giant footer wordmark rejection; a row of four cards. Take only the checklist card.
